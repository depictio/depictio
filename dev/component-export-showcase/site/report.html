Amplicon surveillance of surface water — SLU-SEEC

Demonstration page. Not an official Swedish Pathogens Portal page. It reproduces the portal's dashboard layout, and every figure, table, number and control below is backed by Depictio's component-export API instead of the portal's own Django views. The data is the nf-core/ampliseq reference dataset seeded into this Depictio instance, so the biology is real but the survey it describes is not a Swedish monitoring programme.

Survey at a glance

Twelve water and soil samples were collected across four habitat types (groundwater, riverwater, sediment and soil) over three weeks in January 2023, and the 16S rRNA gene was amplified and sequenced for each. The four figures below summarise what came back: how many habitat groups are represented, how many bacterial and archaeal phyla were detected across the whole survey, how many distinct taxa those phyla resolve into, and how relative abundance is distributed once every taxon in every sample is pooled.

Read the last one first. Amplicon relative abundance is heavily right-skewed: a handful of taxa account for most of the reads and a long tail sits near the detection limit. That shape is why the tabs that follow use log scales and pooled "other" categories rather than plotting every taxon at equal weight, and why the abundance filter in the deck above is the fastest way to separate the community's structure from its noise.

These four numbers are Depictio card components. A card has no Plotly specification to hand over, so each arrives as format=html in its own frame rather than as JSON this page draws. That is the honest answer for a card rather than a gap: the manifest publishes which of the two formats each component supports, and this page follows it instead of guessing. Every panel on this page carries a footer saying which of the two it used.

Sequencing quality

Nothing below the Overview tab means anything if the sequencing failed, so the quality report comes first. These six panels are the pipeline's own MultiQC report, exported one module at a time from a different Depictio dashboard (the ampliseq QC tab of the same project) and dropped into this page beside the biology. That is worth noticing: a host page is not restricted to one dashboard's worth of components, and the filter deck above drives both.

The sample and habitat filters reach these panels through the pipeline's sample sheet. The taxonomic filters do not, because a MultiQC module knows nothing about phyla; when that happens the panel's status line says ignored and names the column rather than showing you an unchanged figure as though the filter had worked.

General statistics

One row per sample, the same table MultiQC puts at the top of its report: read counts, duplication, GC content and the fraction surviving trimming. Look for a sample that sits apart from the rest on any column; in a four-habitat survey a single outlier is usually a sampling or extraction problem rather than a biological signal, and it is worth excluding through the Sample ID control before reading the composition tabs.

Sequence counts and read survival

Total reads per sample, and how many of them survived primer removal and quality trimming. Amplicon libraries are shallow by design, so what matters is not the absolute depth but its evenness: relative abundance is a composition, and a sample sequenced far below the others will look artificially sparse in every downstream figure. The paired panel shows the same libraries after Cutadapt, which is where a failed primer set announces itself as a collapse in surviving reads rather than as a quality warning.

Per-base quality and read length

The Phred profile along the read, and the length distribution after trimming. For a 16S amplicon both are expected to be tight: one amplicon length, one library preparation, so a broad or bimodal length distribution points at off-target amplification rather than at natural variation. Quality decaying at the 3' end is normal for Illumina chemistry and is what the trimming step exists to remove; quality collapsing in the middle of the read is not.

Adapter content

The fraction of reads carrying adapter sequence at each position. For an amplicon library this is the panel that reports the failure the previous two only hint at: when the insert is shorter than the read, the sequencer runs off the end of the fragment and into the adapter, and the curve climbs toward the 3' end. A flat line near zero means the amplicon is longer than the read and nothing was read through. A curve that lifts early, or lifts for one sample and not its neighbours, points at off-target amplification producing short products rather than at a sequencing problem.

Sampling and sequencing

Twelve samples span four habitat types and a three-week window in January 2023. The 16S rRNA gene was amplified, sequenced, and processed with nf-core/ampliseq: primer removal with Cutadapt, quality control with FastQC, denoising into amplicon sequence variants, and taxonomic assignment against a reference database. The quality panels on the Overview tab are that pipeline's own MultiQC output, not a reconstruction of it.

Two consequences worth carrying into the other tabs. First, everything downstream is relative abundance: amplicon sequencing measures proportions within a library, not cell counts, so a taxon rising in one habitat means it rose relative to the others in that habitat, not that there is more of it per litre. Second, taxa that cannot be assigned at a given rank are kept and labelled Unclassified rather than dropped, which is why they appear as a real slice in the hierarchy figures. A pipeline that silently discarded them would show a cleaner and less truthful picture.

The sample metadata

Every filter on this page ultimately resolves against this table. It is also the collection that makes cross-collection filtering possible: habitat and sampling date live here, the taxonomy lives in a different collection, and the project's declared link between the two is what lets a habitat filter reach a taxonomy figure at all.

This one is kept as a frame on purpose. It is the same component type as the taxonomy table on the Community composition tab, which this page draws itself from format=data, so the two sit here as a comparison rather than as a rule. A frame brings Depictio's grid with it, filtering and column menus included, and costs a full bundle to do it; the rows cost one request and leave the rendering to the host. Neither is the right answer in general, which is why the manifest reports both and the footers say which one produced what you are looking at.

How the binding works

Each control names a column, and the export manifest publishes which interactive component drives which column, along with its option list. So the deck is never hard-coded against the data: if a column has no control on the dashboard, the deck omits it rather than rendering a widget that filters nothing. Depictio resolves the rest server-side, including the joins between this project's collections, so a filter on sample metadata still reaches a figure drawn from the taxonomy table. When a filter reaches nothing, the response says so and the panel reports it.

The request surface

Everything this page does is four query parameters on one endpoint. Open any panel's How this was fetched footer to see the exact request that produced what is on screen, including the parameters and the response's own provenance block.

format
json for a Plotly specification this page draws itself, html for a self-contained frame Depictio draws.
filters
the array the deck above builds, each entry naming a column and a value.
controls
a component's own view options, for components that have more than one view.
expect_version
pins the dashboard revision, so a re-save cannot silently change a published figure. The versioning page exercises it.

Using Depictio's own control instead

The controls in the deck were rebuilt in this site's idiom from the manifest's filter contract. The alternative is to embed Depictio's real widget as format=html and listen for what it posts out. Both paths end at the same filters= array on the export URL, so a host chooses how much of Depictio's interface it adopts, not whether it can use the data. Pick a sample below and the figures on the other tabs follow it.

Limitations

Twelve samples over three weeks is a snapshot, not a time series, and the sampling date control is included to show that a date range reaches the server rather than because three weeks supports a temporal claim. Habitat groups are small enough that a single unusual sample moves a habitat mean visibly, which is exactly what the Sample ID control is for: exclude one sample and watch whether the pattern survives. Relative abundances are compositional, so the phyla in a habitat are not independent of each other and a rise in one is arithmetically a fall in the rest.

Quantitative data analysis

Mean relative abundance per phylum, faceted by habitat. Each row is one habitat type and each bar one phylum, averaged across that habitat's samples. The plots can be re-generated using the interactive inputs above; kindly refer to the tab for how the survey was processed.

How to read it. The four rows separate into two regimes. The two water habitats are dominated by a single phylum: Proteobacteria account for roughly 65% of both riverwater and groundwater. What differs is the runner-up, and it is a meaningful difference: riverwater's second phylum is Bacteroidetes at about 23%, a group associated with degrading complex organic matter in flowing surface water, while groundwater's is the archaeal Thaumarchaeota at about 11%, ammonia oxidisers characteristic of dark, nutrient-poor subsurface water.

Soil and sediment are the other regime: far flatter, with no phylum above about 25%. Soil spreads across Acidobacteria (about 25%), Proteobacteria (22%), Thaumarchaeota (19%), Verrucomicrobia (11%) and Actinobacteria (10%), which is the classic signature of a structurally complex habitat supporting many metabolic strategies at once. Sediment sits between the two regimes, still Proteobacteria-led at about 40% but with Acidobacteria and Chloroflexi taking a real share. The single most useful thing to do here is to switch the habitat toggle on and step through the four values: the water samples barely change shape, the soil and sediment ones do.

Qualitative composition per sample

The same data without the averaging: one column per sample, stacked by taxon. Taxa below the retention threshold are pooled; empty columns indicate samples with no assigned reads at that rank.

How to read it. A habitat mean is only trustworthy if the samples behind it agree, and this is where that gets checked. Columns from the same habitat should look like variations on one another; a column that does not resemble its neighbours is the sample to exclude with the Sample ID control before reading anything into the habitat it belongs to. The pooled "other" band at the top is worth watching too: a sample whose pooled band is much larger than its neighbours' is a sample whose community was too even to be summarised by its top taxa, which is a real finding rather than a plotting artefact.

The underlying table

The taxonomy table itself, one row per sample and taxon with its relative abundance. It is the collection every figure on this tab is drawn from, and it responds to the same filters.

This one is not framed. A table has no Plotly specification, but it does have rows, and format=data hands those over as {columns, rows, total}. So the markup below is this page's own, in this page's type and alignment, with this page's pager: the same bargain format=json offers for a figure. Compare it with the framed table on the tab, which is the same component type delivered the other way.

Taxonomic hierarchy

The survey as a nested hierarchy: domain at the centre, then phylum, class and order outwards. Both this and the flow diagram below are server-side figure builders, so what arrives is a finished specification rather than a request for this browser to compute one.

How to read it. Bacteria carry roughly nine tenths of the summed abundance and Archaea the rest, which is unremarkable for surface water and soil. The informative ring is the third one. Proteobacteria is the largest phylum overall, but it is not one thing: it splits into Betaproteobacteria (the largest single class in the survey), Gammaproteobacteria, Deltaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria, and those classes have genuinely different ecologies. A figure that stopped at phylum would report "Proteobacteria dominate" for every habitat and hide the distinction. Within the Archaea nearly all of the signal is Thaumarchaeota, and nearly all of that is the order Nitrososphaerales, the ammonia oxidisers already visible in the groundwater column on the previous tab.

Click a segment to zoom into that clade and click the centre to come back out. The Unclassified slice is kept deliberately: at roughly a tenth of the bacterial signal, dropping it would visibly inflate every other group.

Flow from domain to genus

The same hierarchy as a Sankey diagram, which trades the sunburst's sense of proportion for a clearer view of where a clade's abundance actually goes. Follow a band from left to right: a phylum that stays in one thick band is taxonomically coherent in this survey, while one that fans out into many thin bands is a group whose members here are only distantly related to each other. The width of a band at the last rank is also a fair warning about resolution: 16S amplicons often cannot separate genera reliably, so a thin band at the right-hand edge should be read as "assigned here" and not as "confidently identified".

Taxa shared across habitats

An UpSet plot of the taxa each combination of habitats has in common: the bars count taxa, and the dot matrix underneath says which habitats each bar refers to. It answers the question the other figures on this page cannot, which is whether the four habitats are drawing on one regional pool of organisms or on four largely separate ones.

How to read it. Compare the bars over single dots (taxa unique to one habitat) with the bar over all four dots (the shared core). A large shared core with small habitat-specific sets means the habitats differ mostly in the proportions of a common pool, which is what the composition tab suggests for these samples; large single-habitat bars would mean genuinely different communities. Filtering to one habitat collapses the plot to that habitat's own set, which is also the quickest way to confirm the deck above is really reaching the server.

Two dashboards are listed because this page draws from two: the community dashboard supplies the composition figures, the cards, the tables and every control in the deck, and the ampliseq QC dashboard supplies the sequencing panels on the Overview tab.
